Online fitness coaching for people in the Palm Bay–Melbourne–Titusville area

The Palm Bay metro is Brevard County, the Atlantic coast from Palm Bay and Melbourne north to Titusville. More than nine households in ten here have a broadband subscription, and the coastal weather station counts about seventy-six days a year at or above 90 F, with January lows around fifty. Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Brevard County is on Eastern time, the same as me, so the call can go anywhere in your evening.

The CDC’s metro-area survey has no activity or strength-training figure for the Palm Bay–Melbourne–Titusville area for 2023 or later, so none is shown here, and the state’s figure is not used in its place.

Daily life in the Palm Bay–Melbourne–Titusville area, by the numbers

From the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, published value and margin of error together: about 14.6% of workers who live here work from home (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.6); commuters average 25.4 minutes to work (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.4); the median age is 46.7 years (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.1); 93.0% of households have a broadband subscription (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.5). Training through the heat in the Palm Bay–Melbourne–Titusville area

At the MELBOURNE WFO station (USC00085612, 17.7 km from Palm Bay's Census internal point), the 1991–2020 normal July high is 90.9 F, with 76 days a year at or above 90 F on average, an average January low of 50.5 F, and 55.28 inches of rain a year.

The figures come from the Melbourne weather-office station, picked over two stations nearer Palm Bay whose records are shorter. Read them as the pattern for this stretch of coast rather than a reading for your street.

How coaching works from a distance

Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Your workouts live in the Trainerize app, you weigh in twice a week, you send progress photos every two weeks, and we talk on a weekly call scheduled around your time zone. Between calls, you can message me with questions.

July runs just above 90 F here. When does outdoor training work?

Most of the year at almost any hour, and on the hot days at the edges of the day. The station above averages about seventy-six days a year at or above 90 F, roughly one day in five, with July highs just over ninety. On those days a run or a long walk goes to the first hour of light or after sunset, and the lifting stays indoors, where the temperature decides nothing. With January lows around fifty, the cool months are the open season: outdoor sessions can move to lunchtime or the afternoon. The weekly call is where we switch between the two, so a hot week costs a session its usual time, not the session itself.

More than nine households in ten have broadband. What does the weekly call need?

A connection most homes here already have, and a phone if yours does not. The weekly call runs as a video call when the line allows it and as a plain phone call when it does not, and nothing is lost either way, because the workouts live in the app and the two weigh-ins a week and the progress photos need nothing more than your phone. About one worker in seven here works from home, so for some people the call can sit in a quiet part of the workday; for everyone else it takes an evening slot that stays the same from week to week. A fixed time matters more than a fast line: the call that happens every week is the one that keeps the plan on track.
