Online fitness coaching for people in the Ocala area

The Ocala metro is Marion County, in the middle of the state north of Orlando. About one worker in eight here works from home and the average trip to work is around twenty-seven minutes, while the Ocala station counts more than a hundred and ten days a year at or above 90 F. Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Marion County is on Eastern time, the same as me, so the call can go anywhere in your evening.

The CDC’s metro survey and the Ocala area

The CDC’s metro-area survey has no activity or strength-training figure for the Ocala area for 2023 or later, so none is shown here, and the state’s figure is not used in its place.

The Florida page, linked below, has the statewide survey. What this page can measure for Marion County is the week itself: work, the trip there and the weather.

Daily life in the Ocala area, by the numbers

From the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, published value and margin of error together: about 12.0% of workers who live here work from home (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.9); commuters average 26.8 minutes to work (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.7); the median age is 47.9 years (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.2); 91.1% of households have a broadband subscription (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.7). Metro-area figures: your own city may differ.

Training through the heat in the Ocala area

At the OCALA station (USC00086414, 7.3 km from Ocala's Census internal point), the 1991–2020 normal July high is 92 F, with 110.3 days a year at or above 90 F on average, an average January low of 45.6 F, and 51.64 inches of rain a year.

The station is close to the city and all four of its figures carry NOAA’s standard record, so they read the year as it is here: a long hot stretch and a cool one, both worth a plan.

How coaching works from a distance

Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Your workouts live in the Trainerize app, you weigh in twice a week, you send progress photos every two weeks, and we talk on a weekly call scheduled around your time zone. Between calls, you can message me with questions.

Most workers here travel to work. Where do the sessions go?

Into the same slots every week, on either side of work. With only about one worker in eight working from home, most people here travel to a workplace, around twenty-seven minutes to get there on average, so the sessions are planned around the trip and the hours it leaves free. The plan picks two or three fixed slots, usually straight after work before the evening fills up, or before work for people who would rather be done early, and treats them like appointments. A shorter version of each session is written in for the days that run late, so a long shift shortens the workout instead of cancelling it.

More than a hundred and ten hot days and cool January mornings. Does the plan change with the weather?

The outdoor half does; the lifting stays put. On the hot days, more than a hundred and ten a year at the Ocala station, with July highs in the low nineties, a run or a walk belongs in the first hour of light or after dark, and the strength sessions stay inside. In the coolest part of the year the timing turns around: January lows average in the mid-forties, so early sessions start with a longer warm-up and outdoor work moves to the warmer middle of the day. The change is made on the weekly call when the weather turns, so it is planned rather than found out after a week of missed sessions.
