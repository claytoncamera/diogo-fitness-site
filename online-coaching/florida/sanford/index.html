How coaching works from a distance

Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Your workouts live in the Trainerize app, you weigh in twice a week, you send progress photos every two weeks, and we talk on a weekly call — Sanford is on Eastern time, the same as South Florida, so there is no time difference to plan around. Between calls, you can message me with questions.

Some of my workdays are at home and some are not. Where do the sessions go?

On the same days every week, whichever kind they are. About one worker in five here works from home, and the average trip to work is about twenty-five minutes, so the drive itself does not eat the day. What breaks a mixed week is sessions that float: a home day gets one if nothing comes up, an office day gets one if the evening stays free, and by Friday there were none. The plan pins each session to a day and a time, before work on the office days and at lunch or the end of the workday on home days, and the weekly call moves a pin only when the week itself changes. Which workout split fits your week covers how three days divide the work.

More than a hundred days a year at or above 90 F. How does the plan get through them?

By sorting the sessions into the ones that can move and the ones that never do. At the Sanford station, more than a hundred days a year reach ninety degrees or more and the July high is above ninety, so for a long stretch of the year an afternoon outdoor session is the one at risk. The lifting is not, because it is indoors. The outdoor pieces, walks, runs or a bike ride, each carry a hot-day slot with them: before work or after sunset. When the January lows near fifty arrive, the same pieces move to the middle of the day. Planned that way, a heat wave changes a time on the calendar and nothing else.
