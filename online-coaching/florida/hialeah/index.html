Activity and sleep in Hialeah

Age-adjusted, an estimated 37.6% of adults in Hialeah report no leisure-time physical activity (a modeled estimate, 95% CI 33.7 to 41.5). An estimated 38.5% usually sleep less than 7 hours in a 24-hour period (same kind of estimate, 95% CI 34.2 to 43.2).

The estimate is modeled for the whole city, so it says nothing about any one person in it. What it does say is that starting from no activity at all is common here, and a plan for that start is a different plan from one for someone who already lifts.

Daily life in Hialeah, by the numbers

From the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, published value and margin of error together: about 13.4% of workers who live here work from home (90% margin of error plus or minus 1.1); commuters average 29.0 minutes to work (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.6); the median age is 45.8 years (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.6); 86.5% of households have a broadband subscription (90% margin of error plus or minus 1.2).

Training through the Hialeah heat

At the MIAMI INTL AP station (USW00012839, 8.9 km from the Census internal point), the 1991–2020 normal July high is 90.6 F, with 91.4 days a year at or above 90 F on average, an average January low of 61 F, and 67.41 inches of rain a year.

These are the Miami International Airport figures, the same station as the Miami page, so the climate advice is the same as there: lifting indoors all year, outdoor work early or late on hot days, and the mild winter, with January lows around sixty, as the stretch when outdoor sessions can go at any hour.

How coaching works from a distance

Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Your workouts live in the Trainerize app, you weigh in twice a week, you send progress photos every two weeks, and we talk on a weekly call — Hialeah is on Eastern time, the same as me, so the call can sit anywhere in your evening. Between calls, you can message me with questions.

More than a third of adults here report no activity. Where does a plan start?

Smaller than you would guess. The CDC’s model-based estimate above puts more than a third of adults in Hialeah at no leisure-time physical activity, so a first month that starts from no activity at all is a common case here, not the exception. That month is two short full-body sessions a week, five movements, weights light enough that every rep looks the same, and a walk on the days in between at an hour that is comfortable. Nothing is added until those two sessions have happened for a few weeks running. The point of the first month is not a result; it is that there is a second month. How to start lifting weights walks through the first sessions.

We have no internet at home. What do I actually need to be coached online?

A phone with a data plan and a place to train. About one household in seven here has no broadband subscription, and that rules nothing out. Your workouts sit in the Trainerize app on the phone, the two weigh-ins a week are a number you enter there, and the progress photos every two weeks are taken on the same phone. The weekly call can run on mobile data, so the one thing worth planning is when it happens: a fixed time, somewhere your signal is good. The plan itself is written for the space you have, a gym or a living room, not for the equipment you do not.
