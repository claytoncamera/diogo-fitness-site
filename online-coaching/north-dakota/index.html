Online fitness coaching for people in North Dakota

North Dakota has one of the smallest shares of people working from home on these pages and one of the shortest commutes, a median age below the middle, and one of the lower household broadband shares, with a share of adults who lift a little above it. It is young, in the office, and close to work. Coaching for North Dakota is about the hours after a short drive, through a long winter. Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. North Dakota is on Central time, an hour behind me, except its southwest corner, which keeps Mountain time.

How North Dakota trains, per the CDC's state survey

In 2023, 41.2% of adults in North Dakota reported muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week (BRFSS, 95% CI 39.5 to 42.9). In 2025, 22.9% reported no leisure-time physical activity at all (BRFSS, 95% CI 21.5 to 24.3).

Daily life in North Dakota, by the numbers

From the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, published value and margin of error together: about 8.1% of workers who live here work from home (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.3); commuters average 17.9 minutes to work (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.3); the median age is 35.9 years (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.2); 88.7% of households have a broadband subscription (90% margin of error plus or minus 0.4). State figures: your own city may differ.

How coaching works from a distance

Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Your workouts live in the Trainerize app, you weigh in twice a week, you send progress photos every two weeks, and we talk on a weekly call scheduled around your time zone. Between calls, you can message me with questions.

Nobody here works from home. Is online coaching for people like us?

It is for anyone who can reach a gym or a garage two or three times a week, and a short-drive week can. North Dakota has one of the smallest work-from-home shares on these pages and one of the shortest commutes, so the plan never assumes a midday hour: it assumes a session at the end of the day, two or three times a week, forty minutes each, with the weekend carrying the longest one, and it names the smallest version for the day the shift ran over. What is online is the coaching, not the training: the program in the app, the weigh-ins, the weekly call in your evening. What online fitness coaching actually is explains the split.

Winter here is six months and thirty below. What is the plan?

Indoors, from day one, so the season never gets a vote. The lifting happens in a gym, a garage or a basement all year and the sessions do not change with the calendar; the walking moves to a treadmill, a hallway or a rink concourse, with a floor you can hit on the days the car will not start. The state survey says a little under a quarter of adults in North Dakota report no leisure-time activity, and the winter is where most of that number is made. The weekly call runs on the connection most households here have; if the video is poor, we talk. A storm week is a shorter week, not a lost one. Consistency without motivation is the idea.

I already lift some. What changes with a coach?

The program gets a direction. The state survey puts North Dakota a little above the middle of these pages for adults lifting two or more days a week, and the median age here is on the younger side, which describes a lot of people who train and plateau: the same lifts, the same weights, a new program every couple of months found online. Coaching adds enough weekly work for each muscle, a progression you can see in the logbook from one week to the next, and someone who reads the log every week and changes what needs changing. The energy is already there; the plan gives it somewhere to go. Coaching to build muscle is the program.
