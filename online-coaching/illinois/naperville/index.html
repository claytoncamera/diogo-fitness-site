How coaching works from a distance

Diogo is based in South Florida and coaches online. Your workouts live in the Trainerize app, you weigh in twice a week, you send progress photos every two weeks, and we talk on a weekly call — for Naperville that is Central time, an hour behind me, which puts the call comfortably in your evening. Between calls, you can message me with questions.

I split my week between home and the office. How does the plan handle that?

It gives each kind of day its own job. The figures above say nearly a third of workers in Naperville work from home and the mean commute is over half an hour, so if your week mixes home days and office days, each kind of day gets its own job. The harder, longer sessions go on home days, before the first call or at lunch, and office days get a short session before you leave or a walk at lunch. When your office days move, the weekly call moves the sessions, so the program follows the week you actually have instead of the one on a template.

I’m over forty. Does the program change?

The structure stays and the progression gets more careful. Naperville’s median age is over forty, and the CDC’s estimate above says about one in six adults here report no leisure-time activity. Those are figures about the city, not about you; what matters is where you are starting from. The program adds a rep or a set before it adds weight, keeps most sets a few reps short of failure, and treats recovery days as part of the plan. If something hurts in a sharp way rather than a tired way, see a doctor first; I am a fitness coach, not a doctor.
