Strength training for soccer players: what the trials in players measured, the lifts, and a week around matches

Soccer is ten kilometers of running broken up by sprints, jumps, tackles and turns. Here's what the research in players measured, what lifting and jumping changed, the lifts, a pre-season and in-season week, and the no-weights version.

Short answer: Lift heavy twice a week in pre-season and once a week in season, with jumps and sprints beside it. In 21 elite players with no comparison group, eight weeks of heavy half squats and intervals twice a week went with a 51.7% higher half-squat max and a 10-meter sprint 0.06 seconds faster.3

A 2005 review of soccer physiology sets out the job: elite players run about 10 kilometers in a 90-minute game at an average intensity close to the anaerobic threshold, 80 to 90% of maximal heart rate, and inside that, the game asks for repeated explosive bursts: jumping, kicking, tackling, turning, sprinting, changing pace, and holding forceful contractions to keep balance and the ball under pressure.1 Strength training for soccer players is aimed at those bursts. In 17 international male players, maximal half-squat strength was strongly correlated with sprint times and jump height.2 That's a correlation in elite players, not proof the squat caused the speed, so the training trials below matter more.

What lifting did in trials of players

  • Eight weeks of pre-season. 21 players from a top-level elite team, aged 20 to 31 and new to maximal strength training, did half squats with maximal loads, 4 sets of 4 reps, and aerobic intervals at 90 to 95% of maximal heart rate, twice a week for eight weeks beside their usual training. Their half-squat max rose from 116 to 176 kilograms (51.7%), their 10-meter sprint time dropped by 0.06 seconds, their jump rose 3.0 centimeters and their maximal oxygen uptake rose 8.6%. There was no comparison group.3
  • Seven weeks, with a comparison group. Professional players added heavy strength training, some of them with jumps as well, twice a week for seven weeks to 6 to 8 soccer sessions a week; a control group only played. The lifters improved more than the controls in half-squat max and in two jump tests, but not in sprint acceleration, top sprint speed or 40-meter time, where the difference between groups wasn't significant.4
  • Across 24 studies. A 2015 review of 523 players concluded that strength training improves jumps more than running actions, and acceleration over 10 meters more than top speed over 40, and that professional players need large strength gains for slight improvements in sprinting and changing direction.5

In these studies, lifting made players stronger and helped their jumps more than their sprints, and the sprint gains were smaller and less consistent. That's why the week below keeps sprints and jumps beside the lifting.

How heavy, and how often

Heavy, in two reviews: high loads, few reps and maximal effort on the way up developed strength in one,6 and the other found high-intensity resistance training seems more efficient than moderate-intensity training aimed at muscle size.5 On frequency, the 2015 review found two sessions a week enough to raise force and power in pre-season, and one a week enough to avoid losing it in season.5

Jumps have their own evidence. A 2020 meta-analysis of 17 randomized trials in adult male players found plyometric training improved jump height, 20-meter sprint speed and endurance, but not strength, sprint speed over other distances or agility, and rated the trials' quality as low.7 The jump list: plyometric exercises.

Common questions

What are good workouts for soccer players at home, without weights?

Jumps and sprints need no weights, and jumps are what the plyometric meta-analysis tested.7 Add, practically, single-leg squats and lunges made harder with slow reps, Nordic curls with a partner holding your heels, and side planks. The bodyweight workout has a full no-equipment session. Heavy squats still need a gym.

What should soccer players do in the gym?

The table above: heavy squats first, the hamstring and single-leg work after, jumps beside them. Twice a week in pre-season and once in season.5

Should soccer players lift during the season?

Once a week, in the 2015 review, was enough to avoid losing what pre-season built.5 Keep it short, heavy and early in the week.

Will lifting make me slower?

Not in these studies. Squat strength went with faster sprints in elite players,2 sprint time improved in the eight-week pre-season program,3 and in the seven-week trial the lifters' sprinting didn't improve significantly more than the controls'.4 Keep sprinting in the week either way.

Lifting around team training and matches is a scheduling problem as much as a training one.
