Shoulder press machine: the setup, how it differs from a barbell press, and what the studies measured

Sit down, hold the handles, press. The machine sets the path, so you don't balance anything. A shoulder press machine is an overhead press with the path set for you. You sit against a pad, take the handles, and press them up; the weight stack or plates move on a fixed track. That's a description of the equipment, not a study result.

This page covers the setup, what a muscle-activity study found when it compared the machine press with the barbell press, what focusing on a muscle changed, what eight weeks on machines produced, and the sets. The free-weight versions are on the overhead press and the dumbbell shoulder press.

How to use a shoulder press machine

These steps are practical setup, not study results. Every machine differs a little, so follow its placard.

  1. Adjust the seat so the handles start near shoulder height, with your elbows a little below the handles.
  2. Sit back against the pad, feet flat on the floor, and take the handles. If the machine has a choice of grips, a neutral grip (palms facing each other) is the one used in the study below.
  3. Press up until your arms are straight, without arching your back off the pad.
  4. Lower slowly back to the start, one rep per press.
  5. Pick a weight from the stack with a rep or two left at the end of the set, and write the number down. It's a record for that machine only, since machines differ.

Machine against barbell

In 8 male competitive bodybuilders, about 30 years old with about ten years of training, the front machine shoulder press with a neutral handgrip allowed a heavier one-rep max than the front barbell press, about 95 against 82 kilograms. The barbell press produced more activity in some of the muscles measured, with the chest and upper trapezius not differing, and the authors say it takes more work to keep the bar's path steady.1 It isn't a verdict that either press is better. The sample was eight bodybuilders, so the authors caution against extending it to other groups.1

For the barbell press and the studies that measured it, see the overhead press.

Does thinking about a muscle change it?

On a machine shoulder press, 31 healthy young adults with no resistance-training experience lifted 40% of their max in three conditions: no focus, a focus on the shoulders, and a focus on the triceps. Focusing on the shoulders raised shoulder activity, focusing on the triceps raised triceps activity, and upper trapezius activity didn't change.2 That's muscle activity in one session in beginners, not growth. Mind-muscle connection covers the rest of the studies.

What eight weeks on machines produced

In 38 resistance-trained men, an eight-week machine program (a full squat, bench press, prone bench pull and shoulder press on machines) and a free-weight program of the same exercises had similar strength changes, and the cross-sectional areas measured increased in both with no significant differences between them.3 The muscles measured were the thigh, the chest and the abdomen, so the trial doesn't speak to shoulder growth by itself.

Sets, reps and where it goes

  • Practical start: 3 sets of 8 to 12, stopping with a rep or two in the tank. (How to judge reps left.) The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 position stand, which covers healthy adults, advises at least two sets per exercise.4
  • As your vertical push. It can take the place of a barbell or dumbbell press on a shoulder day, or follow one as a second press. The shoulder workout shows where it goes.
  • Pair it with a pull, such as a row, and a side-of-the-shoulder move like the lateral raise.
  • Add weight in the smallest steps the stack allows. When every set reaches the top of the range, go up one plate. Progressive overload covers the options.

Common questions

Shoulder press machine or dumbbells?

No study cited here put the machine press beside the dumbbell press. In the one that put it beside the barbell press, the machine allowed a heavier max and the barbell produced more activity in some of the muscles measured.1 The dumbbell press is on its own page.

Is the shoulder press machine good for beginners?

The path is set for you, so there's less to balance. As practical advice, start with a weight you can press for 12, and ask staff to show you the seat adjustment. I'm a fitness coach, not a doctor: if you have a shoulder, neck or other health condition, ask your doctor or a physical therapist before you press overhead.

What muscles does it work?

In the bodybuilder study, activity was recorded in the shoulder, upper trapezius, chest and triceps muscles, and the barbell press produced more activity in some of the muscles measured, with the chest and upper trapezius not differing.1 In the focus study of 31 untrained adults, activity was measured in the deltoid, the triceps and the upper trapezius during a machine press.2 Those are activity measurements in single sessions, not a ranking of what the machine builds.

Can I build muscle on machines only?

In the trial of 38 resistance-trained men, a machine program that included the shoulder press and a free-weight program raised strength and grew the thigh, chest and abdominal muscles measured by similar amounts.3 A full machine session is on the machine-only workout plan.
