Lat pulldown form: grip, the pull, and the road to a pull-up

Sit under the bar, grip a little wider than your shoulders, and pull it to the top of your chest. Most of the questions people have are about the grip, and the research says it matters less than people think. Here's the setup, the pull, the faults, the variations, and how the pulldown leads to a pull-up.

Short answer: Thighs locked under the pad, hands one to two shoulder-widths apart, palms away. Pull the bar to the top of your chest, elbows driving down toward your back pockets, then let it rise until your arms are straight. Grip width changed lat activity little in a study of 15 men.3 Lean back slightly, no more.

The lat pulldown is a vertical pull: you sit under a cable machine and pull a bar down from overhead to your chest. It's the pull-up's motion with a weight you choose, so you can train that pattern before you can lift your own body weight, and keep using it long after.

This page covers the setup, how wide to grip and which way your palms face, the pull itself, the faults (mostly leaning back and swinging), the close-grip and standing versions, and how the pulldown leads to a pull-up. Where it fits in a session is on the back workout.

The setup

  1. Set the thigh pad so your thighs are snug under it with your feet flat on the floor. It keeps you in the seat once the weight on the stack gets close to your body weight.
  2. Stand, grip the bar with your hands one to two shoulder-widths apart, palms facing away, thumbs around the bar.
  3. Sit down with the bar, arms straight overhead, and settle under the pad.
  4. Chest up, a slight lean back. Just enough that the bar can travel down in front of your face to your chest.

The pull, step by step

  1. Start from straight arms, with your shoulders lifted toward your ears by the weight.
  2. Pull your shoulder blades down first, a small movement before your elbows bend.
  3. Drive your elbows down toward your back pockets and pull the bar to the top of your chest.
  4. Let the bar rise under control until your arms are straight again. That's one rep.

In front, not behind your neck. In 14 resistance-trained men, the front pulldown produced greater overall activity in the main pulling muscles than pulling behind the neck.1 A 2026 review of 23 studies of lat activity called the front bar path the most consistently supported setup on the pulldown.2

Grip width and hand position

This is where the advice is loudest and the evidence is quietest.

  • Width matters little. In 15 men, lat activity was similar with grips one, one and a half and two times shoulder width, and the narrow and medium grips allowed slightly heavier six-rep maxes than the wide one.3 In 40 men with at least five years of training, seven pulldown versions that differed in grip type, width and torso angle produced no significant difference in lat activity.4
  • One older study disagrees. In 10 healthy men, a wide grip pulled to the front produced more lat activity than a close grip, an underhand grip or a wide grip behind the neck.5 The 2026 review sums the grip-width findings up as inconsistent.2
  • Palms away or toward you. In 12 men, palms facing away produced more lat activity than palms facing you, at both a narrow and a wide width.6 In another study of 12 men holding each pull still, palm direction made no difference to lat activity on the pulldown.7

So pick a width between one and two shoulder-widths, wherever you feel strongest and can pull through the full range, and use either palm direction. All of this is muscle activity measured during a few reps, not growth over months, and almost all of it is in men.

The common faults, and a fix for each

  • Leaning far back. A slight lean is fine: in the 40-man study, a 30-degree lean didn't change lat activity, though it raised activity in the back of the shoulder.4 Past that, the pulldown turns into a row, and usually into a swing.
  • Momentum. Rocking back to start the pull and letting the stack drop between reps. The weight is moving you. Go lighter until the torso stays still and the bar rises in about two seconds.
  • Half reps. Arms straight at the top, bar to the top of your chest at the bottom.
  • Pulling with your arms. Common, and partly fixable with a cue. In eight women with little or no lifting background, instruction on using the lats raised lat activity while biceps activity didn't change.8 Think of your hands as hooks and lead with the elbows.
  • Shrugging the bar down. Shoulders up at your ears for the whole set. Pull the shoulder blades down before the elbows bend.
  • Pulling through pain. Sharp pain is not soreness. Stop, and see a doctor or a physical therapist.

Lifting straps? In 12 trained men, straps didn't change the one-rep max, the reps done at 70% of it or lat activity on the pulldown.9 Use them if your grip gives out first; they don't add anything otherwise.

VersionHowWhat changes
Wide-grip pulldownOverhand, about one and a half to two shoulder-widthsThe standard version; the grip studies above
Close-grip pulldownA V-handle or a narrow bar, palms facing each otherElbows stay closer to your sides
Underhand pulldownPalms facing you, about shoulder widthMore like a chin-up; slightly less lat activity in one study
Single-arm pulldownOne handle, one arm at a timeEach side works alone
Standing or kneeling cable pulldownA high cable, no thigh padLighter, because nothing holds you down
Straight-arm pulldownStanding at a high cable, arms nearly straightThe elbows barely bend, so the arms do less of the work

The close-grip and neutral-grip versions are a matter of what feels strongest: in the 40-man study, a narrow neutral grip sat among the seven versions with no difference in lat activity.4 Pick one, and keep it long enough to add weight to it.

The pulldown as a path to a pull-up

The pulldown trains the same pulling motion as a pull-up, with less than your body weight. What it does and doesn't do:

  • It builds the pull. In 29 untrained young men, ten weeks of pulldowns grew the elbow flexors about as much as ten weeks of curls (6.10% against 5.83%).10 The pulldown is a back lift that trains the arms too.
  • It raised pull-up reps in 34 recreationally active male college students who did pulldowns three times a week for eight weeks, on a normal machine or an unstable setup. All of them could already do at least one pull-up, so the trial says nothing about getting a first rep.11
  • The numbers don't line up one to one. In 58 college students, 35 men and 23 women, the heaviest single pull-up, with weight added or counterweighted, was more weight relative to body weight than the heaviest pulldown, in both groups.12 So pulling your full body weight on the stack isn't the entry ticket to a first pull-up.

Use the pulldown alongside the parts it doesn't train, such as negatives and hanging from the bar. How to do a pull-up has the full route from zero to one, with the pulldown as one of four ways in.

Sets, reps and where it goes

  • Two or three sets of 8 to 12, with a rep or two in reserve. (How to judge reps left.) The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 position stand advises at least two sets per exercise and found strength was enhanced for exercises done at the beginning of a session.13
  • Early in a pull or upper session, if it's the pull you most want to improve.
  • Pair it with a row, such as the dumbbell row or the seated cable row: the back workout has a slot for each direction.
  • Add weight when every set reaches the top of the range with reps to spare. (Progressive overload covers how much.)
