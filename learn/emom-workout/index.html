EMOM workout: every minute on the minute, what the studies found and three to run

An EMOM is a clock rule: start a set at the top of every minute and rest for whatever is left. Here's what the format is, what the studies that compared it with AMRAP found, three sessions to run, and how to set the work so the rest is real.

Short answer: EMOM means every minute on the minute: start a set at the top of each minute and rest for what's left of it. In a study of 12 athletes, an EMOM had the lowest heart rates of the three timed formats tested.1 As a practical rule, pick a set that takes 20 to 40 seconds so the rest stays real.

An EMOM is a clock rule, not an exercise. At the start of every minute you do a set; whatever time is left in the minute is your rest. A faster set earns a longer rest, a slower one a shorter rest, and the minute never moves. That's the whole idea, and it's why an EMOM keeps a session on schedule.

This page covers what the format is, what the studies that compared it with other timed formats found, three EMOMs to run, how to set the work so the rest is real, and where it fits in a week. The other timed format, as many rounds as possible, is on the AMRAP workout.

What an EMOM is

A 2024 study describes the format as performing the specified repetitions within a one-minute interval, with work and rest both happening inside each minute. In that study, the athletes did 10 repetitions of each exercise inside its minute, then rested until the next minute began.1 A 2025 study describes the same thing as a pace per round, with the chance of more recovery if you finish faster.2

What the studies found

Four studies compared an EMOM with an AMRAP and a for-time workout, all in single sessions:

  • Heart rate and bar speed. In 12 athletes with at least a year of experience in this style of training, 10 men and 2 women, an EMOM, an AMRAP and a rounds-for-time workout of squats, pull-ups and shoulder presses were done on separate days. The EMOM had the lowest heart rates of the three, bar speed fell least within sets in the EMOM (significantly less than in rounds-for-time), and the AMRAP had the lowest bar speeds.1
  • Three load levels. In 25 participants with over a year of experience, grouped by how heavy the load was for them, the EMOM again had the lowest heart rates, and the AMRAP showed higher velocity losses than the EMOM in the lightest-load group (under 40% of one-repetition maximum).3
  • The same work, three ways. In 25 healthy men and women with at least three months of experience, the same volume was done as an AMRAP, a for-time workout and an EMOM. Blood lactate was lowest after the EMOM, jump height fell after the AMRAP and the for-time workout but not after the EMOM, and the more experienced group rated the EMOM the least demanding.2
  • Speed lost across a workout. In 9 trained men doing front squats and shoulder presses, the loss in lifting speed across the workout was 73.2% in the AMRAP, 61.6% in the EMOM and 76.1% for time.4

In all four, the EMOM was the least fatiguing, or had the lowest heart rate, of the formats tested; the authors of the 2024 study say the lower heart rate is likely attributable to the structured rests built into the EMOM.1 All four studied single sessions: athletes with at least a year of experience in this style of training in two, participants with at least three months in the third, and trained men in the fourth. None says what weeks of EMOM training do.

Three EMOMs to run

These are practical starting points, not protocols from a study. Warm up for five easy minutes first, and finish with a few easy minutes.

SessionWhat you do each minute
Bodyweight, 12 minutesOdd minutes: 10 bodyweight squats. Even minutes: 8 push-ups. Six rounds of each
Weights, 15 minutesThree-minute cycle, five times. Minute 1: 10 goblet squats. Minute 2: 8 push-ups. Minute 3: 12 kettlebell swings
Bike or rower, 10 minutes20 seconds hard at the top of every minute on a bike or a rower, easy for the other 40 seconds

Set the reps so a set takes 20 to 40 seconds. If you can't finish inside the minute, you've found the ceiling: do fewer reps next time rather than letting the minute run over. To progress, add a rep or two per minute, then add a few minutes, then add load or a harder version when sets start running past 40 seconds.

Setting the work so the rest is real

The format hides one thing: a set that takes 20 seconds leaves 40 seconds of rest, and one that takes 45 leaves 15. What the rest needs to be depends on what you're training:

  • Muscular endurance. The American College of Sports Medicine's 2009 position stand recommends light to moderate loads of 40 to 60% of one-repetition maximum, more than 15 repetitions and rests under 90 seconds.5 The rest fits an EMOM; the sessions below use 8 to 12 reps, so they are practical starting points, not that prescription.
  • Heavy strength. The same stand recommends 3- to 5-minute rests for heavy loading of 1 to 6 repetitions.5 A review of rest intervals found robust strength gains even with rests under 60 seconds, though it seems longer rests, over two minutes, are needed to maximize strength gains in resistance-trained people; for untrained people, 60 to 120 seconds looked sufficient.6 An EMOM isn't the tool for heavy sets. Rest between sets covers the longer waits.
  • Two moves alternating. The same exercise comes up every second minute, so the rest before it repeats is two minutes minus the set: about 100 seconds for a 20-second set. That is arithmetic, not a finding, and it's why the first two sessions above alternate moves.

Where it fits in your week

The World Health Organization's guideline for adults includes muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days a week, plus 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75 to 150 of vigorous.7 A 10- to 15-minute EMOM works as a short session on its own, or as the last part of a lifting day after the heavy sets. As a practical start, do one a week, away from a heavy leg day. Supersets covers another way to cut the waiting out of a session.

Common questions

How long should an EMOM be?

Ten to fifteen minutes is a practical start. The 2024 study's AMRAP ran for 12 minutes.1

EMOM or AMRAP?

An EMOM fixes the rest and the AMRAP leaves it to you. In the studies above, the EMOM left people less fatigued at the same work. The AMRAP workout covers the other format.

Can an EMOM build strength?

No study here tested weeks of EMOM training. In the rest-interval review, robust strength gains came even with rests under 60 seconds, and for untrained people 60 to 120 seconds looked sufficient;6 that is about rest between ordinary sets, not EMOMs. For heavy strength, use longer rests and a regular set structure.

What if I can't finish the reps inside the minute?

Drop the reps by a few next session. The clock is the point of the format; stretching the minute turns it into something else.
