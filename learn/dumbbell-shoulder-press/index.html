Dumbbell shoulder press: seated or standing, and pressing without the lean

A dumbbell at each shoulder, pressed to straight arms overhead, sitting on a bench or standing on the floor. Each arm works on its own, and the standing version asks the most of your balance. Here's how to do both, what the studies found each one trains, dumbbells against kettlebells, the faults, and the sets.

Short answer: Hold a dumbbell at each shoulder, palms forward or turned slightly in, brace, and press until your arms are straight over your shoulders. Lower to shoulder height. The dumbbell shoulder press is the overhead press with a dumbbell in each hand. You start with the weights at your shoulders and press them to straight arms above your head, either sitting on a bench with an upright back or standing. Each arm moves on its own, so a weaker side can't hide, and the dumbbells can travel a slightly different path from a bar, which doesn't have to clear your face.

This page covers both versions, what the studies measured in each, dumbbells against kettlebells, the faults and the sets. The barbell press, its bar path and grip width are on overhead press form; lowered behind the head instead, the bar press becomes the behind-the-neck press, which its study's authors write asks for more shoulder mobility.1 Start with your palms facing you and turn them as you press, and it's the Arnold press.

How to do a dumbbell shoulder press

  1. Get the dumbbells to your shoulders. Seated, rest them on your thighs, then kick them up one at a time as you sit tall. Standing, curl or clean them up to your shoulders.
  2. Set the start: the dumbbells at shoulder height, just outside your shoulders, palms facing forward or turned slightly in, forearms upright, elbows a little in front of your body.
  3. Brace. Ribs down, glutes tight if you're standing, a breath held. Seated, keep your back against the pad.
  4. Press up until your arms are straight and the dumbbells sit over your shoulders, close together at the top.
  5. Lower under control to the start, about shoulder height, and press again.

Standing, press from a stance about hip width, knees straight but not locked. If your knees dip to get the weights moving, you've turned it into the push press, a different lift.

Dumbbell shoulder press muscles worked

The press works the front and side of the shoulder most, with the triceps straightening your elbows; the upper trapezius works too. In 12 trained and 12 novice women, the dumbbell shoulder press produced more front-shoulder and upper-trapezius activity than an incline or a flat dumbbell press, and less activity in the middle of the chest than either.2 The back of the shoulder does less; a press day needs a pull, such as face pulls, for that. Raising the dumbbells straight in front of you instead of pressing them is the front raise: in 10 competitive bodybuilders it drew more upper-chest activity than four lateral raise versions, and more front-shoulder activity on the way up.3

The tool and your position change the details. In 15 healthy men tested at 80% of their max, a barbell produced about 15% less front-shoulder activity than dumbbells standing and about 11% less seated, while drawing about 39% more on the triceps standing.4 The full dumbbell-or-bar comparison is on dumbbell vs barbell.

Standing or seated

In the same 15 men, the standing dumbbell press produced the most shoulder activity of the four versions and the lowest max: about 7% less than the standing barbell press and about 10% less than the seated dumbbell press. Seated, the dumbbells drew about 15% less activity from the side of the shoulder and about 24% less from the back of it than standing.4

  • Standing asks more of your balance and your brace, works more of the shoulder, and lets you lift less. It's the version for a home setup with no bench.
  • Seated with your back against an upright pad takes the legs and most of the balance out, so you can press more weight. It's the simpler place to learn the press and the easier one to add weight to.

Neither is the right one. Pick the version you can repeat with good reps, and keep it for a few months so the weight can go up.

On an exercise ball

Sitting on an exercise ball to press doesn't add much. In 16 healthy men pressing at 80% of their max, the dumbbell shoulder press on a ball and on a bench produced no significant difference in front-shoulder, chest or abdominal activity.5 The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 position stand also found strength was not affected by training on unstable versus stable surfaces.6 A bench, or standing on the floor, is the simpler choice.

Dumbbell or kettlebell

Two small studies tested the same weight as a dumbbell and a kettlebell. In 21 healthy college-aged students pressing one arm overhead at a quarter of their machine-press max, the dumbbell produced more front-shoulder activity than a kettlebell of the same weight, 63.3% against 57.9% of a maximal contraction.7 In 28 male college baseball players pressing the same 11.3 kilograms, side-shoulder activity didn't differ significantly between a dumbbell and a kettlebell held the usual way; holding the bell bottom-up lowered it compared with the dumbbell.8 So use whichever you have. With a kettlebell, the bell rests on the back of your forearm at the start; press it the same way.

Does it build the shoulders?

No trial that measured shoulder growth from the shoulder press turned up in a PubMed search for this page, so the studies here are activity in a single session, not growth over weeks. What they show is that a press is a front-and-side exercise. The side of the shoulder also has a training trial of its own on lateral raises, and the shoulder workout puts the press, the raise and a pull together in one session.

The common faults, and a fix for each

  • Leaning back. The press turns into a steep incline press. Ribs down, glutes tight standing, back on the pad seated; lighten the dumbbells if you can't stay upright.
  • Elbows flared straight out at the bottom. Keep your forearms upright and your elbows a little in front of your body.
  • Half reps. Bring the dumbbells down to about shoulder height and press to straight arms. The ACSM's 2026 stand found strength was enhanced by training through a complete range of motion.6
  • Clanking at the top. The dumbbells finish close together over your shoulders, not knocked together over your head.
  • One arm racing ahead. Press both at the same speed and stop the set when the weaker arm slows down.
  • Pressing through something that hurts. Sore muscles the next day are normal; a sharp or lasting hurt in the shoulder isn't. Stop, and see a doctor or a physical therapist.

Sets, reps and where it goes

  • Three sets of 8 to 12, stopping with a rep or two left. (How to judge reps left.) The ACSM's 2026 stand, which covers healthy adults, advises at least two sets per exercise.6
  • First on a shoulder day, or as the vertical push on an upper-body day. The same stand found strength was enhanced for exercises done at the beginning of a session.
  • Count it with your other presses. If it comes after a chest day of bench pressing, the front of your shoulder has already worked; two sets is usually plenty.
  • Move up by reps, then by pair. A step up in dumbbells is a big jump on a press, so reach the top of your rep range on every set first. (Progressive overload covers the options.)
