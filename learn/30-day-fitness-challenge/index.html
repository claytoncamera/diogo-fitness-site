30-day fitness challenge: a beginner calendar you can print

Thirty days, four movements, a walk three days a week and a rest day every week. It's a month for building the habit and learning the movements, written out day by day, with a PDF to stick on the fridge.

Short answer: Three short bodyweight sessions a week (squat, glute bridge, push-up, plank), a brisk walk on three other days that grows from 20 to 50 minutes, and one rest day. Two sets each for the first two weeks, three after that. It's a habit-and-skill month, not a promise about your body.

This 30-day challenge is a small version of a real training week: a short bodyweight session three times a week, a walk on three other days, and a day off. Nothing to buy, nothing to track except what you did.

Be clear about what a month can do. This challenge is about two things: making training a normal part of your week, and learning four movements well enough to keep going after day 30. It doesn't promise any change in weight, size or shape, and no honest page can tell you what thirty days will do for your body.

The week the month repeats

  • Three strength days (days 1, 3 and 5 of each week): four bodyweight movements for the legs, hips, chest and arms, and core.
  • Three walk days (days 2, 4 and 6): a brisk walk, the kind you'd rate about a 5 or 6 on a 0 to 10 effort scale, which is how the World Health Organization describes moderate activity.1
  • One rest day (day 7): your normal day. There's always a day between the strength sessions, so no muscle trains hard two days running. (Rest days explains why.)

The WHO guidelines ask adults for 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening work for all major muscle groups on two or more days a week.1 They also say some activity is better than none, and that people not yet meeting the guidelines should start with small amounts and build up over time.1 So the walks start below the guideline on purpose: 60 minutes in week 1, 90 in week 2, 120 in week 3 and 150 in week 4, the bottom of the range.

The strength session

Warm up with one easy set of the squat and the push-up at about half your reps. Then do the four movements in order, resting about a minute between sets:

MovementStart hereMake it harder when it's easy
Bodyweight squatSit to a chair seat and stand upA lower step, then a full squat, then a slow lowering
Glute bridgeBoth feet downSingle-leg glute bridge
Push-upHands on a kitchen counterHands on a bench or stair, then the floor
PlankHands or forearms on a counter, or short holds of 10 to 15 secondsForearms and toes on the floor

Print the 30-day calendar (PDF).

  • Reps: 8 to 20 a set. Pick the version where you can do at least eight good reps. More than twenty with reps to spare, move to the harder one.
  • Plank: hold only as long as your body stays in a straight line. Once a 45-second hold feels easy, make the version harder rather than holding longer.
  • Weeks 1 and 2: two sets of each, stopping with three or four reps left. The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 position stand advises at least two sets per exercise.2
  • Weeks 3 and 4: three sets, stopping with one to three reps left. (Here's how to judge reps left.) Going all the way to failure isn't needed: the same stand found it doesn't add gains in strength or muscle.2

The easy first two weeks are deliberate. Soreness is common when a kind of activity is new, and one review advises bringing new activities in gradually over a week or two.3

A 2016 meta-analysis of 138 studies, about goals in general rather than exercise, found that monitoring progress helped people reach their goals, and helped more when the progress was physically recorded.4 Day 29 next to day 1 shows what you learned: which version you're on and how many good reps it takes. Four rules for the month

  • Missed a day? Do it tomorrow. Slide the rest of the calendar back a day. The month is the thirty days in order, not the dates on a calendar.
  • Never miss twice. One missed day is life. Two in a row is how a month quietly ends.
  • Don't add more. Feeling good in week 1 is the plan working. Keep the rest day and the reps left; the third set comes in week 3.
  • If it hurts, stop. Sharp pain is not soreness. If you have an injury, a medical condition or a medical question, talk to a doctor before you start. I'm a fitness coach, not a doctor.

What to do after day 30

The point of the month is that you keep going. Three ways to carry on:

  • Stay at home with no equipment: the bodyweight workout keeps these four movements and adds the one this month leaves out, a pull (a row).
  • Move into a gym: how to start lifting weights is the first four weeks with weights.
  • Not sure which: the workout plan quiz asks six questions about your week and points to a plan, and all the free plans are on one page.

Keep the walks too. Week 4's 150 minutes is the bottom of the WHO's range, not the top.1

Common questions

Is this a weight-loss challenge?

No. It's a training habit and four movements. It doesn't promise any weight, size or shape change, and no page can promise what a month will do for your body. If food is the question, calories and protein is the plain version.

Can I do it if I've never exercised?

Yes. It's written for that. Every movement has an easier version to start on, the first two weeks stop well short of failure, and the first walks are twenty minutes. If you have an injury or a medical condition, check with a doctor first.

Why not train every day?

Because hard sets for the same muscles every day is the part to avoid, and the rest days are when your body answers the sessions. Walking most days is fine. Rest days has the research.

Can I swap in other exercises?

Keep the four slots and swap within them: a reverse lunge for the squat once it's easy, a dead bug for the plank. The calendar works because each session covers the same four slots, not because of the exact exercises.
