Walking is the most underrated tool for fat loss

It isn't flashy, and it won't go viral. But steps add to what you burn every day without costing you a workout. Here's how many to aim for, what they burn, and how to make them stick.

Short answer: Don't jump to a big step count. Start from the steps you already average, add about 2,000 a day and hold it until it feels routine, then raise it. In studies of pedometer programs, people added about 2,200 to 2,500 steps a day, and having a step goal was an important predictor of the increase.1

Nobody posts a thumbnail of themselves walking. It's not intense, it's not new and it doesn't come with a discount code. It also quietly adds up.

If you're lifting and eating sensibly, steps are the easiest extra to add: they add to what you burn each day and they don't need a gym. As a practical matter, a walk is gentle enough for most people to fit around their training. This page covers how many steps to aim for, what extra steps burn, and where walking fits next to lifting and food.

How many steps a day for fat loss?

There isn't one number that works for everyone, so start from your own. A practical routine:

  1. Find your baseline. Check your phone's step count for a normal week and average it.
  2. Add about 2,000 steps a day to that average and hold it for two weeks.
  3. Move up again when it feels routine, by another 1,000 or so.

The 2,000 is about what pedometer programs delivered. A systematic review of 26 studies with 2,767 participants found that people using a pedometer raised their activity by about 2,500 steps a day more than control groups in the randomized trials, and by about 2,200 over their own starting point in the other studies, across programs that ran 18 weeks on average.1 The review also found that having a step goal, such as 10,000 a day, was an important predictor of the increase.1 So set a number, write it down, and check it.

What 3,000 extra steps burn

Here's the arithmetic, using two research numbers:

  • Pace: in a study of 76 healthy adults aged 21 to 40, about 100 steps a minute was proposed as the threshold for moderate intensity, 3 METs.2 So 3,000 steps at that cadence take about 30 minutes.
  • Energy: one MET is about 1 calorie per kilogram of bodyweight per hour.3 Thirty minutes at 3 METs is about 1.5 calories per kilogram.

That's about 105 calories in total at 70 kg (154 lb) and about 135 at 90 kg (198 lb). The total includes the resting energy you'd use anyway, about 1 calorie per kilogram per hour,3 so the extra from the walk itself is closer to 70 and 90. Either way it's an estimate, and people differ. The walking calorie calculator works it out for your own weight, time and pace.

Easy ways to get more steps

These are practical habits, not study protocols:

  • Walk after meals. Even 10 minutes. The meal is a built-in reminder.
  • Take calls on foot. If you don't need a screen, you don't need a chair.
  • Park farther away and take the long way to the door.
  • Split it up. Three 10-minute walks beat zero 30-minute walks.
  • Make it pleasant. A podcast, a playlist, a route you like. If it feels like a punishment, you won't keep it.

Does walking replace lifting or good nutrition?

No. Walking supports the main work; it doesn't replace it.

  • Lifting builds and protects muscle, which shapes how you look and how you perform.4,5
  • Nutrition matters more than steps for weight: in trials, programs built on activity alone led to less weight loss than programs that also changed eating.6 Calories and protein covers the food side.
  • Walking adds to what you burn each day and keeps you moving between workouts.

Doing harder cardio too? Here's how to fit it around lifting. Walking uphill on a treadmill is its own page: the 12-3-30 workout. And if you're weighing walking against running, running vs walking compares them.

The simplest version (a practical routine)

Pick a floor you can hit even on a bad day, like 6,000 steps. Hit it every day for two weeks. Then raise it by 1,000. That's the whole plan.

Common questions

How many steps a day to lose weight?

No single count does it on its own. In trials, activity-only programs led to less weight loss than programs that also changed eating,6 so steps work best alongside food. Start from your average and add about 2,000 a day.

Is 10,000 steps a day necessary?

No. It's a common goal, and in the pedometer review a step goal like it predicted bigger increases,1 but the number that matters is one above your average that you'll actually keep.

How many calories do 3,000 steps burn?

At about 100 steps a minute, roughly 1.5 calories per kilogram of bodyweight in total: about 105 at 70 kg and 135 at 90 kg, of which about 70 and 90 are the extra from walking. The calculator does your own numbers.

Is walking better than running for fat loss?

Running vs walking has the study numbers. Either works if you keep doing it.
