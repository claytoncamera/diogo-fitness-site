What the first month of online coaching actually looks like

People picture a PDF and a weekly text. Here's what the first four weeks with me actually involve, week by week, including the part most people find hardest.

Short answer: The first month of online coaching is mostly questions, a plan sized to your real week, your first weigh-ins read as a trend, a weekly call where we change one thing, and the week-three dip where structure matters more than motivation.

By Diogo·Updated October 26, 2026·4 min read

Coaching with Diogo, online. A training plan built around your real week, a weekly call and check-ins in between.

When people ask how online coaching works, they usually picture a PDF and a weekly text. Here's what the first four weeks with me actually involve, so you can decide whether it's worth it before you apply, and so there are no surprises if you do.

This is my process. Other coaches run theirs differently, which is a good question to ask any of them. (Here's the full checklist.)

Before day one: the conversation

It starts with a short application and a call. The call is mostly questions: your goals, your current routine, what you've tried before, what got in the way, and what your week honestly looks like, commute included (here's the average in your state). If I'm confident I can help you, I show you how the coaching works and what I'd recommend. If I'm not the right coach for you, I say so.

Nothing in the first month works without this part. The plan is built from your answers, not from a template.

Week 1: the plan, the app, the first numbers

Your workouts arrive in the Trainerize app: the exercises, the sets, the reps, in order, built around the days you said you could train. The first sessions are deliberately lighter than you'd pick for yourself. You'll be learning the movements and the app at the same time, and soreness is common when you're first introduced to a kind of activity; one review advises easing into new exercise over a week or two.1

You start the two weigh-ins a week, and we talk about food the way the coaching page describes it: calories and protein as tools, with simple meal ideas and no forbidden foods. The point of week one isn't a result. It's a baseline and a routine.

Week 2: the first call

By now there's something to look at: a week of logged workouts and a few weigh-ins. On the call we look at what happened, not what was supposed to happen. Which sessions got done, which weights felt easy, where the week fell apart. Then we change one thing.

This is where self-monitoring starts to pay. A review of 22 studies found a consistent link between self-monitoring and weight loss,2 and a meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies found that monitoring progress toward a goal helped people reach it, with larger effects when the progress was reported to someone or physically recorded.3 The weekly call is both: you record it, and someone reads it.

Week 3: the dip

Week three is where most solo attempts end. The novelty is gone, the results aren't visible yet, and life has produced its first genuinely bad week. If you've started and stopped before, you know this moment.

Two things are different with a coach. First, the plan already has a floor in it: the smallest week that still counts, so a bad week is a smaller week, not a skipped one. Second, someone notices. In a randomized trial of novice adults, people who got weekly video coaching sessions did more days of resistance training at four weeks than a group given education only.4 The honest footnote is that the difference faded by week eight in that study, which is exactly why the structure continues past month one.

Muscle is already changing in week three, even if you can't see it: in one study using a high-intensity program, measurable increases in muscle size showed up after only about three weeks.5 That's a reason to keep going, not a promise about you.

Week 4: the first trend

Four weeks of weigh-ins is enough to see a direction instead of a daily number. Your first progress photos sit next to the plan. Most lifts have gone up a little. The call looks at the trend and we decide what the next block looks like: more volume, a new exercise, a different split, or the same plan because it's working. (How long results take, honestly.)

What people find hardest

  • Weighing in twice a week. Not the number; the habit of reporting it. It gets easier once you've seen it read as a trend instead of a verdict.
  • Doing less than they want to in week one. The people who last are the ones who trusted the easy start.
  • Messaging when a week goes wrong. The instinct is to go quiet and come back when it's fixed. The whole point is to message me when it isn't.
  • Believing the plan survives a bad week. It does, because it was built from your real week in the first place. (How that works.)

What the first month isn't

It isn't a transformation. In a meta-analysis of 10 studies and 3,899 people, nearly half of the people who intended to meet physical-activity guidelines didn't follow through;6 the first month is where we close that gap for you, not where the mirror changes. No outcome, timeline or weight change is promised, in month one or any other.

If what's missing in your training is structure, feedback and someone who notices, this is what the first month gives you. If you're not sure it's worth paying for, here's how I'd decide. If you are, apply and we'll start with the questions.
