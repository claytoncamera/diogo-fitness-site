Winter cold by metro: January lows in 25 big metros

How cold a January night runs at a named NOAA weather station in each of 25 large metros, and the one part of a training week that winter actually moves.

Short answer: NOAA's 1991–2020 normals put the average January low between 5.5 F (Minneapolis-St. Paul) and 61.0 F (Miami) at principal-city weather stations in 24 large U.S. metros, and 11 of the 24 average below freezing. In those months, move the daily walk indoors and keep the lifting plan exactly as it is.

Winter doesn't have to change your lifting. What it changes is the walk: the daily steps that were easy in May get hard when it's dark at the hour you'd take them and cold the moment you step outside. How much that matters depends on where you live, and NOAA puts a number on it: the average January low at each weather station, from its 1991–2020 climate normals.1

The table takes one station for each of 25 of the largest U.S. metro areas: a station within 25 km of the Census Bureau's internal point for the metro's first-named city, preferring one with a complete record. That makes each row a reading at one spot near that city, not a metro average, so the station's name and distance are printed with it.

What stands out

  • Minneapolis-St. Paul is the cold outlier. Its station (U Of Mn St Paul) averages a 5.5 F January low, the coldest of the 24 stations and 12.1 degrees below the next (Denver-Stapleton, 17.6 F).
  • Eleven of the 24 stations average a January low below freezing: Minneapolis-St. Paul, Denver, Detroit, Chicago, Boston, St. Louis, Baltimore, Philadelphia, New York, Washington and Charlotte, the last at 31.8 F, just under 32.
  • North and south aren't the whole story. Atlanta's station averages a 35.6 F January low, colder than Seattle's (37.2 F) and Dallas-Fort Worth's (37.9 F). Miami's 61.0 F is the warmest of the 24; Tampa (52.8 F) and Los Angeles (51.9 F, at the UCLA station) follow.
  • Close calls and a tie. Detroit (19.4 F, a partial record) and Chicago (19.5 F) are a tenth of a degree apart; Phoenix and San Diego tie at 46.0 F.

How to read your own station's row

A January low is the cold end of a typical January day, so it says the most about the hours a walk before or after work usually falls in. A low below 32 F on average means a typical January morning there starts below freezing. A normal is a 30-year average, not a forecast, and it's measured at the station, not at your door.

What winter changes in a training week

  • The walk moves indoors. When it's dark and cold at walking time, take the steps somewhere warm and lit: a treadmill, an indoor track, laps of a big building, or a walking session at home. Same daily target, different place.
  • The lifting doesn't change. Same days, same sessions, same progression as in summer. Winter is a reason to move the walk, not to pause the plan.
  • Get to the gym on purpose. In a cold month the hard part is leaving the house. Pack the bag the night before, go straight from work, or train at home on the worst days with a three-day dumbbell plan.
  • Keep a floor. When the weather wins a week, a 30-minute session keeps it from going to zero. Never miss twice holds in January too.

Where the numbers come from

NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information publish the U.S. Climate Normals for 1991 to 2020. The January low is the January value of the normal daily minimum temperature from the Monthly Climate Normals.1 Each value was read from the station's own normals file, with NOAA's completeness flag and years of record, on October 22, 2026, and checked the same day by a second, independent re-fetch: every value, flag and year count matched.

How the station was chosen: the metros are the 25 largest metropolitan statistical areas by the Census Bureau's 2024 population estimates. For each, up to six stations within 25 km of the Census Bureau's internal point for the first-named principal city were considered, preferring one with standard flags on every value; the distance in the table is from that point. A station describes its own spot, and normals apply less well the farther you are from the station. San Francisco has no station within 25 km of its point, so it has no figure here.

If you reuse the table, cite NOAA: Palecki, M., Durre, I., Applequist, S., Arguez, A., Lawrimore, J. (2021). U.S. Climate Normals 2020: U.S. Monthly Climate Normals (1991-2020). NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information. A link back here is appreciated.
