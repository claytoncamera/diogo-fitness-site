Strength training for golfers: what the trials in golfers measured, the lifts, and a golf workout week

Club head speed is where the gym meets the golf course. Here's what strength and power training did to speed and distance in trials of golfers, which physical qualities track with a faster swing, the lifts, a week around your rounds, and the at-home version.

Short answer: Lift two days a week: squats, deadlifts, presses and fast rotational throws. A 2026 meta-analysis of 26 controlled studies in golfers found physical training added about 1 m/s of club head speed and 9 m of carry distance on average, though it rated the evidence low certainty.2

A golf workout, in the research, is strength and power training added to golf to make the swing faster. Club head speed is the measure every review below reports, and one trial calls it a major determinant of drive distance.1 This page sticks to what the trials in golfers measured, and how a golfer can build a week from it.

The widest look is a 2026 meta-analysis of 33 studies and 798 golfers of any sex, age and playing level, with programs of at least four weeks. In the 26 controlled studies, physical training improved club head speed by 1.05 m/s, ball speed by 2.20 m/s, carry distance by 9.33 m and total distance by 10.55 m on average. The authors rated the certainty of that evidence low and asked for better-reported studies.2 Two earlier systematic reviews found the same direction: average increases of 4 to 6.4% in club head speed, ball speed and distance where results were significant, across 25 studies that compared training with a control or comparison group,3 and a positive effect on club head speed and hitting distance across 20 studies of resistance training.4 It's a page about what golfers can add, not a promise about your next drive.

What tracks with a faster swing

A 2024 meta-analysis of 20 studies, in golfers of any skill level with elite youth golfers among them, looked at which physical qualities go with club head speed with a driver. Lower-body strength, upper-body strength, jump displacement and power, and upper-body explosive strength were all significantly associated with it; flexibility and balance weren't. Jump impulse had the strongest association, and upper-body explosive strength a noticeably larger one than upper-body strength.5 In 41 elite golfers, touring professionals among them, driver club head speed in the men was very strongly associated with trunk rotation power, jump impulse and power, and peak force in an isometric pull from mid-thigh; in the women it correlated strongly with jump impulse and peak force, isometric bench press force and trunk rotation power.6 These are correlations: they show what faster swingers have, not that training one quality will add speed by itself.

What strength and power training did

One trial set heavy barbell lifting against a lighter routine. Twelve college golfers, six men and six women averaging 20 years old, did eight weeks of either a strength and power program of high-load barbell lifts or a bodyweight and rotational routine. Against the bodyweight group, the barbell group improved average club head speed, squat, power clean and jump height more; peak club head speed didn't change significantly.1

Another ran 10 weeks in 11 high-level university golfers: six weeks of strength training twice a week, then four weeks of power training twice a week, with no control group. The men showed large but not significant improvements in carry distance and ball speed; the women showed significant gains in upper-body force and a large but not significant increase in carry distance.7

General lifting or golf-specific moves? The 2021 review found that combining general strength training with golf-specific training averaged a 4.1% increase in club head speed and 5.2% in hitting distance, against 1.6% and 4.8% for general strength training alone, and that eight-week programs with golf-specific movements at high speed, 3 to 4 sets of 5 to 15 reps, were the most effective.4 That's one review's comparison, so the week below keeps both kinds.

A golf workout week

A practical layout, not a protocol from a trial. Two sessions a week match the university trial;7 start with strength, then shift toward power, as it did.

DayGolfLift
MondayOff or puttingStrength: squats, bench press, rows, single-leg Romanian deadlifts
TuesdayRangeNone
WednesdayOffNone
ThursdayOff or short gamePower: jumps, medicine ball throws, woodchoppers, deadlifts
WeekendRoundsNone

Keep the heavy legs and the throws a day or more away from a round you care about. The full-body workout plan covers how two whole-body days fill a week.

Golf workouts at home

Without a bar or a cable stack, keep the jumps and the rotation. A practical home version: split squats and single-leg Romanian deadlifts with a loaded backpack, push-ups, jumps, and rotational throws against a wall if you have a ball, or banded rotations if you have a band. The resistance band workout covers the band, and the home gym workout plan shows what a pair of dumbbells adds.

Common questions

Does strength training add distance?

In the controlled studies of the 2026 meta-analysis, carry distance rose about 9 m and total distance about 10.6 m on average, with low-certainty evidence.2

Should golfers lift heavy?

In the one trial that compared them, high-load barbell lifting improved average club head speed more than a bodyweight and rotational routine.1 The 2021 review found general strength training did more when fast golf-specific moves were added to it.4

Isn't golf fitness mostly flexibility?

Not by the 2024 meta-analysis: flexibility wasn't significantly associated with club head speed, while strength and power were.5 Keep the mobility you need to make your swing; the trials above that added club head speed trained strength and power.

How long until it shows?

The trials ran eight and 10 weeks,1,7 and the meta-analysis included programs of four weeks or longer.2
