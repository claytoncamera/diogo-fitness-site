AMRAP workout: as many rounds as possible, what the studies found and three to run

An AMRAP is a clock rule too: pick a short list of moves, set a timer, and go round as many times as you can. Here's what the format is, what the studies that compared it with EMOM found, three sessions to run, how to score and pace one, and where it fits in a week.

Short answer: AMRAP means as many rounds as possible: set a clock, usually 10 to 20 minutes, and repeat a short list of moves with fixed reps, counting rounds. In studies that compared formats, athletes paced themselves slower in an AMRAP and ended it more fatigued than in an EMOM.1,2 Start slower than you want to.

An AMRAP is a clock rule: pick a short list of moves, set a timer, and go round the list as many times as you can before it rings. Your score is the number of rounds and extra reps. Next time you try to beat it, or do the same score with cleaner reps. That's the whole format, and the catch is that you choose the pace.

This page covers what an AMRAP is, what the studies that compared it with other timed formats found, three AMRAPs to run, how to score and pace one, and where it fits in a week. The other timed format, a set at the top of every minute, is on the EMOM workout.

What an AMRAP is

A 2024 study describes the AMRAP approach as completing the maximum number of specified repetitions or rounds within a set time limit, over periods of 10 to 20 minutes. In that study, the athletes did 10 repetitions of each exercise and as many rounds as possible in 12 minutes, setting their own pace.1 A 2025 study describes it this way: in an AMRAP the athletes perform the maximum repetitions and they are the ones who split their breaks, so everyone gets equal work time but the intensity and volume depend on the person.2

What the studies found

Four studies compared an AMRAP with an EMOM and a for-time workout, all in single sessions:

  • Bar speed. In 12 athletes with at least a year of experience in this style of training, the AMRAP had the lowest bar speeds of the three formats. The authors put it down to the AMRAP's longer duration, which led the athletes to a self-regulated tempo.1
  • Speed lost. In 25 participants with over a year of experience, grouped by how heavy the load was for them, the AMRAP showed higher velocity losses than the EMOM in the lightest-load group (under 40% of one-repetition maximum).3
  • The same work, three ways. In 25 healthy men and women with at least three months of experience, the same volume was done as an AMRAP, a for-time workout and an EMOM. Jump height fell after the AMRAP and the for-time workout but not after the EMOM, and the EMOM had the lowest blood lactate.2
  • Speed lost across a workout. In 9 trained men doing front squats and shoulder presses, the loss in lifting speed across the workout was 73.2% in the AMRAP, 61.6% in the EMOM and 76.1% for time. The authors concluded that the AMRAP and for-time formats produced the highest mechanical fatigue.4

Read together: the AMRAP left people more fatigued than the EMOM, and because the format leaves the pace to you, pacing is the skill. All four studied single sessions: athletes with at least a year of experience in this style of training in two, participants with at least three months in the third, and trained men in the fourth. None says what weeks of AMRAP training do.

Three AMRAPs to run

These are practical starting points, not protocols from a study. Warm up for five easy minutes first.

SessionOne round
Bodyweight, 10 minutes5 push-ups, 10 bodyweight squats, 10 mountain climbers (count each knee)
Weights, 15 minutes10 kettlebell swings, 8 goblet squats, 6 dumbbell rows each arm
Low impact, 12 minutes8 step-ups each leg, 12 glute bridges, 6 dead bugs each side, 5 push-ups with your hands on a counter

How to score: your score is the number of full rounds plus the reps you finished in the round you were in when the clock ran out, so "5 rounds + 8". Write down the workout, the time, the score and how it felt. Repeat the same workout in a few weeks and compare. Keep the reps fixed for each move, so a score is a count of rounds; pick reps you can do cleanly even when you're tired.

How to pace an AMRAP

In the 2024 study, the AMRAP appeared to produce what the authors call a "stable pacing" pattern: athletes deliberately controlled their effort and kept a consistent relationship between speeds within and between sets, while in the for-time format they strove to go as fast as possible.1 That's the pattern to copy:

  • Start slower than you want to. Pick a pace you could hold for the first three rounds without stopping.
  • Even rounds beat a fast first round. If your last round takes far longer than your first, the first was too fast. Next time, start a little slower.
  • Break before form breaks. The clock keeps running while you rest, so a planned breath is cheaper than a sloppy rep. Stop the workout if form is gone, not when the clock ends.

AMRAP sets in lifting

"AMRAP" also shows up in lifting programs as one set taken to as many reps as possible. That's a different use of the word. The American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 position stand says that while training to failure is not obligatory for optimizing results, there isn't enough evidence to set exact reps-in-reserve targets.5 Reps in reserve covers how to judge it.

Where it fits in your week

The World Health Organization's guideline for adults includes muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days a week, plus 150 to 300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity or 75 to 150 of vigorous.6 A 10- to 15-minute AMRAP works as a short session on its own, or after the heavy sets on a lifting day. As a practical start, do one a week, away from a heavy leg day, and leave a day or two before the next hard session. The HIIT workout covers the structured-interval version.

Common questions

How long should an AMRAP be?

The 2024 study describes AMRAPs of 10 to 20 minutes and ran its own for 12.1 Ten to twelve is a practical start.

Is an AMRAP the same as HIIT?

Not automatically. HIIT is defined by hard efforts with recovery;7 an AMRAP is defined by a clock and a count. The 2024 study's authors say that, because athletes could regulate their own pace, this could point to these formats not being high-intensity modalities.1 How hard it is depends on the moves and how you pace it.

AMRAP or EMOM?

An AMRAP leaves the rest to you; an EMOM fixes it. In the studies above the EMOM was the less fatiguing of the two. The EMOM workout covers it.

Can a beginner do an AMRAP?

This is practical advice, not a finding: yes, with a short clock and moves you can do cleanly, eight to ten minutes, three moves, small fixed reps. Add a minute or a rep before you add anything harder.
