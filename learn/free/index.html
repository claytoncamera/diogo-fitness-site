Data tables

Eight CDC, NOAA and Census Bureau data tables for U.S. states, metros and cities: what each holds, its source and vintage, its CSV and how to cite it. Data tables for writers explains each one and how to cite it.

  • How active is your state?: Every state and DC in one table: the share of adults who report no leisure-time activity and the share who lift two or more days a week, from the CDC's state survey. CSV.
  • How active is your metro?: 48 large U.S. metros in one table: adults reporting no physical activity in the past month and adults lifting twice a week, from the CDC's metro-area survey. CSV.
  • How active is your city?: The CDC's PLACES estimates for 27 U.S. cities: adults with no leisure-time physical activity and adults reporting short sleep, with 95% ranges and how to read them. CSV.
  • Summer heat by metro: NOAA's 1991–2020 normals at weather stations in 48 big U.S. metros: days a year at or above 90 F, the average July high, and when to move outdoor training. CSV.
  • Winter cold by metro: NOAA's 1991–2020 average January low at weather stations in 48 big U.S. metros, and what cold, dark months change in a training week: the walk, not the lifting. CSV.
  • Rain by metro: NOAA's 1991–2020 average annual precipitation at a named weather station in 40 U.S. metros, and what a wet day changes in a training week: the walk, not the lifting. CSV.
  • Commute times by state: The Census Bureau's average trip to work and work-from-home share for all 50 states and DC, with margins of error, and where training fits around each. CSV.
  • Commute times by metro: The Census Bureau's average trip to work and work-from-home share for 40 U.S. metro areas, with margins of error, and where training fits around each. CSV.

When free isn't enough

A plan on paper covers the plan and the progression rule. It doesn't notice when you stop, and it doesn't change when your week does.
