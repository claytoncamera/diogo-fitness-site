Fit

Who this is for. And who it isn’t.

This is for you if…

  • You’ve lost weight before and it came back. Usually because the plan was something you could only do for six weeks.
  • You’re tired of extreme diets. You want to keep the foods you love and still move the trend.
  • You want the scale to stop running your mood. Two weigh-ins a week, read as a trend, by someone who isn’t you.
  • You’ll do the boring things. Steps, protein, lifting, sleep. None of it is exciting. All of it works.

Not a fit if…

  • You want a number of pounds by a date. I won’t promise one. Nobody honest will. (Why I don’t guarantee results.)
  • You want a crash diet. My approach is built around sustainable habits, not restriction.
  • A doctor is managing your weight or a condition. Then they lead and I follow their guidance, or I’m not the right person. I’m a fitness coach, not a doctor.

The idea

Fat loss is a few boring things, repeated.

The research is clear about what works and quiet about what’s exciting. Coaching, for this goal, is making the boring things happen on the weeks you don’t feel like it.

01

Food and movement together.

A review of direct comparisons found that programs combining diet and physical activity with behavior support produced more weight loss at 12 months than diet alone, and more than exercise alone at every point measured.1 That’s the shape of coaching: the food side as the coaching page describes it, the training, and someone to answer to. (Why the scale may not be moving.)

02

Lift, so the weight you lose is fat.

Dieting without training costs you muscle along with fat: a review found that diet-induced weight loss reduces muscle mass, and that resistance exercise helps preserve it and improves strength.3 The ACSM’s position stand on physical activity for weight loss is blunt about the trade: resistance training doesn’t add to the weight you lose, but it may increase fat-free mass and the share of what you lose that is fat.2 So the plan has two or three lifting sessions in it, not an hour of cardio a day. (Cardio and lifting together.)

03

Steps and a plan for the weekend.

Daily movement quietly drops when people start dieting, and weekends quietly undo weekdays: in a year-long trial, people on a calorie restriction lost weight on weekdays and stopped losing on weekends.4 We set a step floor you can hit on a bad day, and we plan the weekend instead of pretending it won’t come. (Walking and steps. · The 80/20 approach.)

04

Numbers someone reads.

A review of 22 studies found a consistent link between self-monitoring and weight loss.5 Two weigh-ins a week, progress photos every two weeks, your logged workouts: I read them weekly and we decide what to change. You stop judging yourself by one Monday morning. (How long results take.)

Honest terms

What I won’t tell you, and what I will.

  1. No number by a date

    I lost 35 pounds myself; that’s my number, not a forecast for yours. No outcome, timeline or weight change is promised, here or anywhere on this site.

  2. No banned foods

    No extreme meal plans or unnecessary restrictions. You’ll learn how to make your favorite foods work for you, with room for restaurants, weekends and social events.

  3. A plan you can run yourself

    I’ll explain the reasoning as we go so you understand what you’re doing and why. The goal is that you stop needing a coach. (The FAQ says the same.)

  4. A doctor first when it’s medical

    The WHO’s guidelines say adults with chronic conditions may wish to consult a physical activity specialist or healthcare professional about what’s appropriate.6 If that’s you, start there.

Questions

Questions about weight loss.

Short answers. The rest is on the main questions page.

Is this weight loss coaching?

Yes. On this page, weight loss and fat loss are the same goal. Dieting without training costs muscle along with fat, which is why the plan has lifting in it. No number of pounds and no date is promised (why I don’t guarantee results).

Is this a diet plan?

Not a crash diet. No banned foods and no extreme meal plans: the food side is the one the coaching page describes, with calories and protein as practical tools and room for restaurants and weekends (the 80/20 approach). If a doctor is managing your weight or a condition, they lead.

How do you track progress?

Two weigh-ins a week, read as a trend, progress photos every two weeks, and your logged workouts. I read them weekly and we decide what to change. (How long results take.)
