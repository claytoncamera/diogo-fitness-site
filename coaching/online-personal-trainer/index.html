The weekly loop

What online personal training actually is.

Three jobs, repeated every week. You do the sessions on your own, at your gym or at home; I do the rest.

01

I write your program.

For your goal, your schedule and the equipment you have. It sits in the Trainerize app, with what to do and in what order, so you walk in knowing what today is. Any gym, or at home.

02

I check in every week.

You weigh in twice a week and send progress photos every two weeks. We talk on a weekly call, scheduled around your time zone, and look at what happened: the sessions you logged, the numbers, and how the week actually went.

03

I change the plan, and answer.

When your week changes or progress stalls, the plan changes with it. Between calls you can message me with questions, and I teach you the why and the technique as we go. Send a video of a lift if you want notes.

Fit

Who this is for. And who it isn’t.

This is for you if…

  • You can get yourself to the gym, or you train at home. You don’t need someone standing next to you every session. You need the plan, the order, and someone to ask.
  • You want one person who knows your plan. The same person writes it, reads your numbers and changes it, every week.
  • Your week keeps changing. Work, travel, family: the plan moves with it instead of breaking. (Coaching for a busy week.)
  • You want to understand it, not just follow it. I teach you the why and the technique as we go, so the plan still makes sense when you’re on your own.

Not a fit if…

  • You want someone beside you for every rep. Then a trainer at your gym is the better fit. (The honest comparison.)
  • Something hurts, or a doctor is managing a condition. Doctor or physical therapist first. With their OK, coaching or a trainer at your gym can follow. I’m a fitness coach, not a doctor.
  • You want a promised outcome by a date. Results vary and are never guaranteed, and you should be careful with anyone who promises one.

Questions

Questions about online personal trainers.

Short answers. The rest is on the main questions page.

Is an online personal trainer the same as an online fitness coach?

Here, yes: they are different names for the same service. One person writes your training program, checks in with you every week and changes the plan when your week changes. I call myself a fitness coach. Names don’t tell you what’s included, so compare what each week actually contains, whoever you hire. (The side-by-side table.)

Can a personal trainer train you online?

Yes. Coaching with me is fully online: your workouts live in the Trainerize app, you train at your gym or at home, and we talk on a weekly call scheduled around your time zone. You can message me with questions between calls; send a video of a lift if you want notes. The one thing online can’t do is stand next to you.

Is an online personal trainer worth it?

It depends on which kind of help you’re short of. If you would train on your own once you knew what to do and had someone checking in, a plan written for you and a weekly check-in may be the missing piece. If you need someone in the room to get through a session, a trainer at your gym is the better fit. (Is online fitness coaching worth it? The longer answer, with the research.)

Do online personal trainers work?

A trainer doesn’t do the work; you do. What a trainer can do is make the work easier to keep doing: a plan you can follow, a check-in every week, and changes when progress stalls. I can’t guarantee results. Be careful with anyone who promises that. What the studies on remote and supervised training do and don’t show is on the worth-it page.

How much does an online personal trainer cost?

With me, it depends on the level of support you need and how long we work together. I’d rather talk with you first and then go over the options that actually fit, than hand you a price list for a plan that isn’t built for you.
