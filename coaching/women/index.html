Who this is for. And who it isn’t.

This is for you if…

  • You want to get stronger. A plan that asks for a little more over time, and a way to see whether it’s working.

What the research says

Strength training works the same way for you.

A lot of fitness advice quietly assumes women need a different, gentler kind of training. The studies that compare the two don’t show that.

01

Men and women adapt in much the same way.

A 2020 meta-analysis pooled studies where men and women followed the same lifting protocol. For muscle size there was no significant difference between them, and for lower-body strength there was none either.1 It covered young to middle-aged adults, so that is the group it speaks for.

02

Don’t skip the upper body.

The same analysis found women’s relative upper-body strength gains were larger than men’s. Its authors say it is possible that untrained women have a higher capacity to build upper-body strength, and that more research is needed to know why.1 Pushing and pulling movements belong in your plan.

03

Two days a week is a real start.

The World Health Organization recommends that adults do muscle-strengthening activity at moderate or greater intensity, involving all the major muscle groups, on two or more days a week.3 The American College of Sports Medicine’s 2026 position stand adds that even minimal doses of lifting bring real strength gains.2 (Why two or three days is a real program.)

What about getting bulky? This research measured muscle growth, not looks: on the same program, women’s growth relative to where they started was not significantly different from men’s.1 It does not say women gain muscle faster than men. If your progress is faster or slower than you want, you tell me on the weekly call and we change the plan.

How it runs

Written around your week, not around a label.

The coaching is the same whoever you are. What changes is the plan, because it starts from your equipment, your schedule and your goal.

  1. Your workouts, in the app.

    Every session is in the Trainerize app: the exercises, the order, the sets. With three days a week I usually start people on full body, and the plan fits the equipment you actually have.

  2. Check-ins you can plan around.

    Weigh-ins twice a week, progress photos every two weeks, a weekly call, and messages between calls when something comes up.

  3. A bad week doesn’t break the plan.

    On a busy week I cut exercises before I cut effort: fewer movements, same intent. (How that works.) Progress comes from adding a little over time. (That’s progressive overload.)

Questions

Questions women ask.

Short answers. The rest is on the main questions page.

Will lifting make me bulky?

The research I rely on is about how much muscle grows, not how a body looks, so it can’t tell you what yours will look like. What it found, in a 2020 meta-analysis of studies where men and women followed the same program, is no significant difference between them in muscle growth.1

Can I train at home with dumbbells?

Yes. Tell me what you have and the plan is written around it, whether that’s a pair of dumbbells in the living room or a full gym. Your sessions are in the Trainerize app, with the exercises and their order written out before you start. (A dumbbell plan for home.)

How many days a week should a woman lift?

The World Health Organization asks adults for muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days a week.3 When someone has three days, I usually start them on full-body sessions, and when two is what the week allows, two is still a real start. (How often to train.)

I’ve only done cardio or classes. Can I start with coaching?

Yes. Say so when you apply. I teach the technique as we go: what each lift is for and what it should feel like. If you’d rather have someone beside you for the first sessions, a few sessions with a trainer at your gym make a good start, and online coaching can follow. (The first four weeks of lifting.)

What does a week of coaching look like?

Your workouts sit in the Trainerize app. You weigh in twice a week and send progress photos every other week. We talk on a weekly call, with messages in between. If a week gets crowded, I trim the exercises rather than the effort. (Coaching for a busy week.)
