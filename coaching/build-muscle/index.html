Fit

Who this is for. And who it isn’t.

This is for you if…

  • You train, but you don’t grow. The workouts happen. The logbook doesn’t move. Usually the plan has no progression in it, or not enough work for the muscles you care about.
  • You want a program, not a random workout. The same lifts, repeated and progressed, instead of something new every session.
  • You’ll log your sets. Progression needs a record. Mine live in Trainerize; yours will too.
  • You want to know why. Why these exercises, why this many sets, why we added weight this week and reps last week.

Not a fit if…

  • You want a number of pounds by a date. Nobody honest can promise what your body does. I can promise the plan, the feedback and the adjustments.
  • You want to be told to train six days. Sometimes that’s right for a specific goal. More often the limit is recovery and your week, and the plan respects both.
  • You’re looking for supplements or shortcuts. I coach training and food, in that order of boredom. Nothing is sold here.

The idea

Four things that build muscle. Everything else is detail.

The research here is unusually clear, and most programs ignore it in favor of novelty. Coaching, for this goal, is mostly making sure the boring things happen every week.

01

Enough weekly work.

What matters most is total weekly work: the number of hard sets you do for each muscle. A meta-analysis of the dose-response found that more weekly volume produced more muscle growth.1 The 2026 ACSM position stand says the same: hypertrophy is enhanced by higher volumes.2 So the first thing I check in a plan that isn’t working is how many real sets each muscle gets in a week. (How many sets per week.)

02

Progression you can see.

Your muscles need a reason to change: a little more weight, a rep more, a set more, over time. A 2022 trial found that progressing by adding reps built muscle about as well as progressing by adding load,3 so there is always a way forward, even on a day the weight won’t move. The logbook is where we see it. (Progressive overload, explained.)

03

Effort, not just load.

Heavy or light matters less than people think: a meta-analysis of 21 studies found light and heavy loads built similar muscle when sets were taken to failure, while heavy loads were better for one-rep strength.4 What a set needs is to be hard enough, which is why we track reps in reserve, not just the number on the plate. (How hard each set should be.)

04

Frequency that fits your week.

A 2016 meta-analysis found training a muscle about twice a week beat once; a 2019 update found little difference once the weekly volume is matched, so how you slice it across the days matters far less.5,6,2 So the split is built around the days you actually have, not the other way round. (How often to train.)

How coaching runs it

What changes when someone reads your logbook.

  1. The program

    Built around your goal, your experience and your week, in the Trainerize app: the lifts, the sets, the reps, and the weight from last time, so every session has a target.

  2. The numbers

    You weigh in twice a week and send progress photos every two weeks. For this goal the scale is the least interesting number; the logbook and the photos tell the story. (How long it takes to see it.)

  3. The weekly call

    We look at which lifts moved, which stalled, and what the week allowed, then change one thing: a set here, a rep range there, a swap when an exercise isn’t doing its job. Between calls you can message me with questions.

  4. The food

    Muscle needs material. You’ll learn to use calories and protein to support your goals without overcomplicating food, the way the coaching page describes it. No forbidden foods and no extreme plans.

How soon? In one study that scanned new lifters weekly, real growth became clear around weeks three and four and kept climbing from there.7 That’s a study, not a promise about you. What I can promise is that the plan will have the four things above in it, every week.

Where it starts

Bring your current program.

Apply and tell me what you’re doing now: how many days, which lifts, roughly how many sets, and how long since a lift last went up. That tells me more than any goal statement.

Questions

Questions about building muscle.

Is online muscle-building coaching right for a beginner?

If you’ve never lifted, start with coaching for beginners, which covers the first weeks. How to start lifting weights is the first month on your own.

How many sets a week do I need?

How many real sets each muscle gets in a week is the first thing to check in a plan that isn’t working. How many sets per week has the numbers.

Do I have to train six days a week?

Rarely. Sometimes that’s right for a specific goal; more often the limit is recovery and your week, so the split is built around the days you actually have. (How often to train.) If your week changes every week, coaching for a busy week covers that.
