Fit

Who this is for. And who it isn’t.

This is for you if…

  • You don’t know your way around a gym. You’d rather walk in knowing what today is than guess at a machine.
  • You can get yourself there. You don’t need someone standing next to you every session. You need the plan, the order, and someone to ask.
  • You want to understand it, not just follow it. The why behind each exercise, so the plan makes sense to you in month three.
  • You’ve started before and drifted off. Usually in week two, when the first plan asked too much. (Why that happens.)

Not a fit if…

  • You feel unsafe lifting without someone beside you. Then start with a few sessions with a trainer at your gym. Online coaching can take it from there. (The honest comparison.)
  • Something hurts, or a doctor is managing a condition. Doctor or physical therapist first, then either of us, with their OK.
  • You want the hardest possible start. The first weeks are deliberately easy. That isn’t a downgrade; it’s the part most beginners skip and then quit.

The first weeks

Short, repeatable, hard to get wrong.

A beginner doesn’t need a complicated program. A beginner needs to get through the first month without getting hurt, getting lost or getting bored. So the first weeks are built for that.

01

Two or three full-body sessions.

A squat or leg press, a push, a pull, a hinge, something for your core: five or six exercises, in order, in the Trainerize app. Two sets of each is plenty at first. The American College of Sports Medicine’s 2026 position stand says even minimal doses of lifting bring real strength gains,1 so a short session is not a wasted one. (Why two or three days is a real program.)

02

Weights that feel too easy.

Every set stops with three or four reps still in you. That’s deliberate: most people misjudge how many reps they have left,3 and in one study of 141 people the guesses tended to get better with experience,4 so we don’t ask you to flirt with failure in week one. Soreness is common when you’re new to a kind of activity, and one review of the research advises easing into new exercise over a week or two.2 Easy first weeks are how you stay in the gym for the hard ones. (How to judge reps left.)

03

Technique, taught as we go.

Your program tells you what to do and in what order, and I teach you the why and the technique as we go: what each exercise is for, what it should feel like, what to change when it doesn’t. The goal is that the gym stops feeling like a place where everyone else knows something you don’t.

And it works faster than it looks. In one study, measurable increases in muscle size showed up after only about three weeks of a hard program;5 in another that scanned new lifters weekly, real growth became clear around weeks three and four and kept climbing.6

Month one

What a beginner’s first month actually involves.

An outline of month one, not a prescription. Your real plan is built around your goal, your experience and your week.

  1. Week 1

    You learn the app and the six movements. Two sessions, both short. The job is to leave each one feeling like you could have done more.

  2. Week 2

    Same exercises, so they start to feel familiar. We add a little weight where the top of the rep range felt easy. Your first weigh-ins land, and nobody panics about them.

  3. Weeks 3 and 4

    A third session if your week has room. One or two new exercises, because by now the first six are yours. On the weekly call we look at the logbook and decide what to add next. (That’s progressive overload.)

Where it starts

You don’t need to know anything to start.

Apply and tell me where you are: whether you have a gym, what you’ve tried, what put you off last time. The first conversation is mostly questions, and none of them is a test.

The World Health Organization’s guidelines say some physical activity is better than none,7 and that is the whole bar for week one. If something hurts or a doctor is involved, that comes first; I’m a fitness coach, not a doctor.

If a trainer at your gym would serve you better at the start, I’ll say so.

Questions

Questions beginners ask.

Short answers. The rest is on the main questions page.

Is online coaching good for beginners?

It can be, if you can get yourself to a gym or train at home. Your program sits in the Trainerize app and tells you what to do and in what order. We talk on a weekly call, and you can message me in between. The one thing online can’t do is stand next to you, so if you’d feel unsafe lifting without someone beside you, start with a few sessions with a trainer at your gym first. (The honest comparison.)

Do I need a gym or equipment to start?

No. Tell me what you have and your program is built around it. A floor, a chair and a doorway are enough for a first bodyweight workout, and the American College of Sports Medicine’s 2026 position stand points to reviews, mostly in older adults, in which home-based resistance training improved strength, muscular endurance and balance.1 (A no-equipment plan to start with.)

How do I learn proper form without a trainer next to me?

The first weeks use weights that feel too easy, so you have room to learn each movement. Your program tells you what to do and in what order, and I teach you the why and the technique as we go: what each exercise is for, what it should feel like, what to change when it doesn’t. Bring questions to the weekly call or message me in between, and if you want, send a video of a lift and I’ll send notes back. And if you’d rather have someone watching your first sessions, a trainer at your gym can do that.

How many days a week should a beginner train?

Two or three full-body sessions. The World Health Organization’s guidelines call for muscle-strengthening activities on 2 or more days a week,7 and the American College of Sports Medicine says even “minimal doses” of lifting bring substantial strength gains,1 so even a small dose counts. Start at the lower number; it’s easier to add a day later. (How often to train.)

What if I’ve never lifted before, or I’m nervous about the gym?

Then the first weeks are built for you: two or three short sessions, a handful of exercises written out in order, and weights that feel too easy. You can feel nervous and still go. Write the workout down before you walk in, keep the first visit short and leave when you planned to. Apply and tell me where you are, and we can talk about whether a trainer at your gym is the better start. (Your first day, step by step.)

How long before I see progress?

I can’t give you a date, and no coach honestly can. What you get from week one is a way to see it: you log your lifts in the app, you weigh in twice a week, and we take progress photos every two weeks. For a rough guide, one study of seven young volunteers found that changes in muscle size were detectable after only 3 weeks of hard training,5 and another, of 25 men over 8 weeks, concluded that significant muscle growth likely occurred around weeks 3 to 4.6 Those were small studies on hard programs, so treat them as a guide, not a schedule. (How long results take.)
